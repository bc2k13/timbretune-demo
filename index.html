Audio details

These TimbreTune samples use an offline rendering workflow: source and converted-performance log-mel spectrograms are aligned, interpolated, and resynthesized with BigVGAN. The plug-in and paper evaluation use SLERP conditioning instead.

The conversion guide uses a 0.95 morph, 100 diffusion steps, CFG 0.5, and Auto F0 off. Playback is mono 44.1 kHz, 24-bit WAV, with level matching and at least 1.5 dB of true-peak headroom.

Method

TimbreTune builds on pretrained Seed-VC without retraining. Whisper supplies source content, F0 supplies pitch, and CAMPPlus encodes the two speaker identities.

The morph amount α interpolates speaker embeddings with SLERP and scales the reference log-mel prompt:

eα = SLERP(es, er; α)Pα = αPr

A diffusion transformer generates mel-spectrograms, and BigVGAN produces audio. A local Flask server connects the model to the VST3 / AU plug-in.

In the plug-in, α = 0 bypasses synthesis and the control ends at 0.95. Full conversion at α = 1 is an offline evaluation endpoint. Equal increments do not guarantee equal perceived changes.

Results

The paper’s preliminary study uses five source–reference pairs and 21 settings from α = 0 to 1. Sources span 7.2–44.0 seconds; references span 6.1–14.6 seconds. CAMPPlus and ECAPA-TDNN measure similarity to both inputs.

As α rises, source similarity falls and target similarity increases. The crossover differs between encoders.

SLERP has the highest mean intermediateness at α = 0.5 in this small study.

Embedding similarity does not establish perceptual quality or a general advantage across voices.

What the scores measure

Source and target similarity are cosine similarities between each output’s speaker embedding and the input recordings. CAMPPlus is used inside Seed-VC; ECAPA-TDNN provides an external view.

The reported intermediateness proxy is 1 − |source similarity − target similarity|, calculated per output and then averaged. Higher values mean more balanced similarity to the two inputs, even when both similarities are low.

Informal listening suggested preserved timing, pitch, and phrasing, but some outputs had distortion and the perceived midpoint varied. No controlled listening study was conducted. Proposed improvements include guidance tuning, export headroom, and smoother chunk overlaps.

The listening samples use the offline rendering workflow described in Audio details. The results here evaluate the plug-in’s SLERP method.

Responsible use

TimbreTune is designed for creative timbre blending. Use self-recorded or explicitly consented reference audio. The 0.95 morph limit discourages full conversion but does not prevent a recognizable reference voice or eliminate misuse.
